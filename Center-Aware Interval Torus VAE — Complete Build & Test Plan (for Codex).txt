Center-Aware Interval Torus VAE — Complete Build & Test Plan (for Codex)
This is a step-by-step, small-scale-first development spec for a feature-level VAE that learns a tonal-center space. It compiles everything we discussed: goals, features, losses, models, datasets, milestones, acceptance tests, evaluation, risks, and the T4 server setup. No code; every step is testable on tiny data.
________________


0) Project goal & assumptions
Goal: Learn a latent geometry where excerpts cluster by tonal-center proximity, using distributional (not waveform) features.
Assumptions:
1. “Tonal center” is probabilistic and high-dimensional (not a single frequency), often multi-modal.

2. Order matters less than aggregate interval structure; still, short context (cadences) can disambiguate—so we keep an order-invariant path, with an optional short-context path.

Core features (all differentiable on a circular grid):
   * log-CCF (p(\theta)): pitch-class density over one octave, (\theta\in[0,1)) (rotation = transposition).

   * CICV (g(\delta)=\mathrm{iFFT}(|\mathrm{FFT}(p)|^2)): rotation-invariant interval distribution over (\delta\in[0,1)).

   * Interval Torus (M(\theta,\delta)=p(\theta),p(\theta+\delta)): retains where intervals live (phase).

   * Center-Field (C(\theta)=p(\theta),(W*p)(\theta)): “tonic saliency” via interval supports (unison/5th/4th/±3rds).

Loss suite:
      * z-loss on CICV (interval content; transposition/inversion invariant).

      * phase-loss on aligned toruses (placement).

      * center-loss on center-field mean & dispersion (explicit tonic geometry).

      * Standard VAE reconstruction + KL (with warm-up / free-bits).

3) Milestone A — Feature pipeline (deterministic, unit-tested)
A1. log-CCF (p(\theta))
Inputs: mono audio or ((f,w)) peaks.
Steps: CQT/HPCP-style salience → (\theta=\mathrm{frac}(\log_2(f/f_{ref}))) → accumulate on circular grid (N\in{720,1024}) → light wrapped Gaussian/von-Mises smoothing → normalize (\sum p=1).
Acceptance tests:
               * Octave-equivalent inputs yield identical (p).

               * Transpose by (k) semitones shifts (p) by (k/12).

               * Single harmonic tone near 220 Hz peaks at (\theta\approx 0).

A2. CICV (g(\delta))
Definition: (g=\mathrm{iFFT}(|\mathrm{FFT}(p)|^2)), normalized to 1. Provide folded variant on ([0,0.5]).
Acceptance tests:
                  * Rotating (p) leaves (g) unchanged.

                  * Major triad vs transposed major → same (g). Major vs minor → different (g).

A3. Interval Torus (M(\theta,\delta))
Definition: (M(\theta,\delta)=p(\theta),p(\theta+\delta)). Output full (M), column sums (g(\delta)=\sum_\theta M(\theta,\delta)), and θ-rotation utility.
Acceptance tests:
                     * Column sums match CICV (within tolerance).

                     * Rotating (p) shifts (M) along (\theta) only; (g) stable.

A4. Center-Field (C(\theta))
Definition: (C(\theta)=p(\theta),(W*p)(\theta)) with (W) = mixture of narrow bumps at:
                        * 0 (unison), (\log_2(3/2)) (5th), (1-\log_2(3/2)) (4th), (\log_2(5/4)) (M3), (\log_2(6/5)) (m3), weights e.g. ([1.0, 0.8, 0.6, 0.35, 0.3]).

Compute circular mean (\mu) and concentration (\rho\in[0,1]).
Acceptance tests:
                           * Single-center tone → (C) unimodal, (\rho) high.

                           * Two-center mixture → (C) bimodal, (\rho) moderate.

4) Milestone B — Losses (stable & differentiable)
B1. z-loss (interval content)
Compare normalized CICVs (g_p, g_q). Default L2; also expose JS/KL/Wasserstein on the circle.
Checks:
 Equal up to rotation/inversion → near-zero; distinct fabrics → positive.
B2. phase-loss (placement on torus)
Pre-align best θ-shift via circular cross-correlation (on (p) or torus row-sum).
Loss: ( \min_\phi |M_p - \text{shift}_\theta(M_q,\phi)|_F^2 ). Optionally minimize over inversion.
Checks:
 Rotated copies → ~0 after alignment; z-related yet phase-different → positive.
B3. center-loss (tonic geometry)
From (C_p,C_q) compute ((\mu_p,\rho_p),(\mu_q,\rho_q)).
Loss: (\min(\rho_p,\rho_q)\cdot [1-\cos 2\pi(\mu_p-\mu_q)]).
Checks:
 Same center → ~0; adjacent (circle-of-fifths neighbors) → small; far → larger; low-(\rho) down-weighted.
B4. VAE regularization
Implement KL warm-up (β from 0 to target) and free-bits per latent dim.
Checks:
 KL per-dim > 0; mutual information not ~0 on toy set (no collapse).

Inputs (configurable):
                              * 1D: CICV (g(\delta)) (interval-invariant baseline), or Center-Field (C(\theta)).

                              * 2D: Torus (M(\theta,\delta)); optionally add (g) as a broadcast second channel.

Concatenate into latent; same losses.

8) Milestone F — Stability & guardrails (monitoring + quick fixes)
Watch for:
                                                   * Posterior collapse: KL→0, z looks like noise.
Fix: stronger encoder / weaker decoder, free-bits, slower KL warm-up, small contrastive term on (z).

                                                   * Symmetry drift (rotation/inversion): inconsistent alignment.
Fix: always align θ before phase-loss; if inversion-invariant, minimize over both orientations.

                                                   * Centers snapping (over-strong (W)): centers stick to a grid.
Fix: reduce (W) weights; add entropy regularizer on (C); cross-validate (W).

                                                   * Pairwise blind spot: miss triadic cues.
Fix: optional tiny triad head (scores for a small set of triad templates) as an auxiliary feature/loss.

                                                   * Front-end brittleness: detuning/timbre noise.
Fix: HPSS, harmonic whitening, slight circular smoothing, detune/EQ augmentation.

                                                   * OOM/slow:
 Fix: AMP on; N=720 first (then 1024); compute torus on-the-fly; reduce batch.

Monitoring: recon, KL per-dim, mutual information proxy, z/phase/center losses, entropy of (p) and (C), (\rho) histogram, VRAM.

Math detail:
Center-Aware Interval Torus — Mathematical Notes (features, properties, and losses)
Purpose
We formalize the mathematics behind the feature set (log-CCF, CICV, Interval Torus, Center-Field), explain their invariances and inter-relations, derive useful identities (especially in Fourier form), and define loss terms and their proper combination for learning a latent “tonal-center space”.
Notation and domain
• θ ∈ [0,1): fractional octave (log-chroma) coordinate, i.e., θ(f) = frac(log2(f / fref)). Arithmetic on θ is modulo 1 (“circular”).
• δ ∈ [0,1): interval size on the same circle (directed when on [0,1), unsigned when folded to [0,½]).
• All integrals over θ or δ are on the unit circle with respect to the uniform measure; in discrete form, sums replace integrals.
• p(θ) ≥ 0 denotes a probability density (continuous) or a probability mass function (discrete) on the circle: ∫0^1 p(θ)dθ = 1 (or ∑i p[i] = 1).
• “⊕” denotes circular addition modulo 1.
A. Logarithmic chroma density (log-CCF)
Definition (continuous). Given any frequency-domain density pf(f) defined on a band, fold all octaves onto a reference octave via θ = frac(log2(f / fref)) and push forward the measure with its Jacobian:
p(θ) ∝ ∑n pf(fref·2n+θ) · ∂θ(fref·2n+θ)
= ∑n pf(fref·2n+θ) · (ln 2) fref·2n+θ.
Normalize p so ∫ p = 1. (Sum over n only where the arguments are in-band.)
Equivalent from linear-within-octave α ∈ [0,1): If f = fref(1+α)2n and you start from plin(α), then
θ = log2(1+α) ∈ [0,1), α = 2θ − 1,
p(θ) ∝ plin(2θ − 1) · (ln 2) · 2θ.
Discrete grid. Choose N bins, θi = i/N. Treat p[i] as a probability mass (nonnegative, sum to 1). A small wrapped Gaussian or von Mises kernel can be used for smoothing.
Key properties.
• Rotation (transposition) by φ: pφ(θ) = p(θ ⊖ φ).
• Inversion: pinv(θ) = p(−θ).
• p is the basic, ambiguity-preserving chroma summary.
B. Circular interval content (CICV)
Definition (continuous). The CICV g is the circular autocorrelation of p:
g(δ) = ∫0^1 p(θ) p(θ ⊕ δ) dθ, with ∫0^1 g(δ) dδ = 1.
Discrete version. With p[i] a probability mass on {0,…,N−1}:
g[k] = ∑i p[i] p[(i+k) mod N], and ∑k g[k] = 1.
Symmetries and invariance.
• Rotation-invariant: pφ ⇒ g unchanged (depends only on interval size δ).
• If you “fold” to unsigned distances, define h(d) on d ∈ [0,½] by
h(d) = g(d) + g(1−d) for 0 < d < ½; endpoints have measure zero (discrete: set h[0]=g[0], h[N/2]=g[N/2] when N even). Then ∫0½ h(d) dd = 1.
Fourier (Wiener–Khinchin on the circle).
Let p(θ) = ∑k ak e^{i2πkθ} (Fourier series). Then
g(δ) = ∑k |ak|^2 e^{i2πkδ}.
Consequences:
• g depends only on |ak| (magnitudes).
• “z-relation” (discrete pitch-class analog): different p with identical {|ak|} share the same g.
C. Interval Torus (phase-preserving pair map)
Definition. The “torus image” M is the joint mass of ordered pairs (anchor θ, partner θ⊕δ) under two independent draws from p:
M(θ, δ) = p(θ) · p(θ ⊕ δ).
Interpretations:
• For each δ, the column M(·, δ) shows where intervals of size δ tend to originate around the circle; bright rows require both anchor and partner to be likely.
• CICV is the θ-marginal of M: g(δ) = ∫ M(θ, δ) dθ (or ∑θ M[θ,δ]).
Conditional anchor distribution given interval. When g(δ) > 0,
p(θ | δ) = M(θ, δ) / g(δ) = p(θ) p(θ ⊕ δ) / g(δ).
Numerically stable variant (discrete):
p[θ | δ] = softmaxθ ( log p[θ] + log p[θ⊕δ] ), which is well-defined even when g is tiny.
Rotation and inversion.
• Rotation by φ acts only along the θ-axis: pφ ⇒ Mφ(θ, δ) = M(θ ⊖ φ, δ). Columns do not change; only row positions shift.
• If desired, define the folded torus (interval-class torus) by summing columns δ and 1−δ (except endpoints).
Fourier factorization of M (key identity).
Let p(θ) = ∑k ak e^{i2πkθ}. Then
p(θ) p(θ⊕δ) = ∑k,m ak am e^{i2π(k+m)θ} e^{i2π m δ}.
Taking a 2-D Fourier transform over (θ, δ) with dual indices (u, v) ∈ ℤ×ℤ yields
M̂(u, v) = a_{u−v} · a_v.
Consequences:
• The δ-marginal (fix v, sum/integrate over θ) collapses to |av|^2 (CICV spectrum).
• M retains the phase structure of p via correlations among ak; CICV discards it.
D. Center-Field (tonic saliency map)
Definition. Choose a small, musically motivated kernel W on the circle that emphasizes stabilizing intervals around a center (e.g., narrow bumps at unison, 5th, 4th, ±3rds with decreasing weights). Define
C(θ) = p(θ) · (W * p)(θ),
where “*” is circular convolution: (W * p)(θ) = ∫ W(δ) p(θ ⊕ δ) dδ.
Intuition: C(θ) is high where (i) the anchor has mass and (ii) its local neighborhood in interval space supports centricity.
Circular statistics of the center-field.
Define the first trigonometric moment (complex mean)
m = ∫ e^{i2πθ} C(θ) dθ / ∫ C(θ) dθ,
μ = arg m ∈ [0,1), ρ = |m| ∈ [0,1].
• μ is the estimated center angle (on the circle).
• ρ is a concentration/confidence measure (1 = perfectly concentrated, 0 = uniform).
For discrete inputs, replace integrals with sums.
Notes on W. W need not integrate to 1; scaling cancels when computing μ, ρ. If W is too peaky or overly weighted on a single interval, C can “grid-snap”; cross-validate W’s weights and widths.
E. Relationships among the objects
• M(θ, δ) = p(θ) p(θ⊕δ) (joint pair map).
• g(δ) = ∫ M(θ, δ) dθ (interval marginal, rotation-invariant).
• p(θ | δ) = M(θ, δ) / g(δ) (anchor distribution given interval).
• C(θ) = p(θ) (W * p)(θ) (tonic saliency through weighted local interval support).
• Fourier: g(δ) = ∑k |ak|^2 e^{i2πkδ}, and M̂(u, v) = a_{u−v} a_v.
F. Losses (precise definitions, invariances, and roles)
All distances below are defined on distributions living on the circle; integration is over the unit interval with wrap-around.
F1. z-loss (interval content; transposition/inversion invariant)
Purpose: penalize differences in interval distributions; be zero for z-related structures (same |ak|).
Definition (L2 default):
Lz(gp, gq) = ∫0^1 (gp(δ) − gq(δ))^2 dδ.
Variants: Jensen–Shannon divergence on the circle; circular 1-Wasserstein (see below).
Invariance: If q is a rotation (and optionally inversion) of p, then Lz = 0.
Circular 1-Wasserstein remark. For probability measures on the circle, W1 can be computed by unwrapping at the best cut point (“circular optimal transport”); this sometimes better reflects small interval shifts than L2.
F2. phase-loss (placement on the torus)
Purpose: retain “where” intervals live (phase/placement) while factoring out transposition.
Alignment. Let shiftφ be the circular shift of M along θ: (shiftφ Mq)(θ, δ) = Mq(θ ⊕ φ, δ). Choose φ∗ that minimizes a discrepancy:
φ∗ = argminφ || Mp − shiftφ Mq ||^2 (Frobenius/L2 over θ,δ).
Loss:
Lphase(Mp, Mq) = minφ || Mp − shiftφ Mq ||^2.
Optional inversion-invariance: also minimize over inversion (θ → −θ), taking the smaller value.
Notes:
• The argmin over φ can be found by circular cross-correlation or FFT-phase tricks; one may treat φ as an external alignment (no gradient through argmin), which is acceptable for training stability.
• Fully differentiable alternative: represent shifts via Fourier rephasing (multiplying ak by e^{−i2πkφ}) and optimize φ as a continuous variable (small grid or a few quasi-Newton steps).
F3. center-loss (explicit tonic geometry)
Purpose: explicitly shape a “tonal-center space” using center-field statistics.
Given Cp, Cq with (μp, ρp) and (μq, ρq), define the center distance on the circle by
dcirc(μp, μq) = 1 − cos(2π(μp − μq)) ∈ [0,2].
Weight by confidence (down-weight ambiguous cases):
Lcenter = min(ρp, ρq) · dcirc(μp, μq).
F4. VAE regularization and reconstruction
Let q(z | x) be the encoder posterior (e.g., Gaussian with diagonal covariance) and p(z) the prior (e.g., standard normal).
• KL term with warm-up: LKL = β · KL(q || p), with β ramped from 0 to a target over training (“KL annealing”).
• Free-bits: enforce a minimum KL per latent dimension (e.g., 0.2 nats) to prevent posterior collapse.
• Reconstruction term Lrec: defined in the same feature space as the input (e.g., L2 or cross-entropy on M or C or g, depending on which is reconstructed).
G. Combining losses (total objective)
For an input example x with features (targets) p, M, g, C and model predictions (decodes) M̂, ĝ, Ĉ (and possibly p̂), the per-sample objective can be:
Ltotal = Lrec(M̂, M) (reconstruct the torus or chosen feature)
+ β · KL(q(z | x) || p(z)) (with warm-up and free-bits)
+ λz · Lz(ĝ, g) (interval content alignment)
+ λphase · Lphase(M̂align, Malign) (placement after best rotation)
+ λcenter · Lcenter(Ĉ, C). (explicit center geometry)
Notes on usage and scheduling.
• Start with λz ≈ λphase ≈ λcenter ≈ 1 (rescale by observed magnitudes); ramp λphase and λcenter only after reconstruction stabilizes, to avoid trivial minima.
• If z-loss dominates, different p with the same g (z-related) may collapse; increase λphase to re-inject phase information.
• If center-loss dominates, centers may “snap” to a grid; reduce kernel weights in W or add an entropy regularizer H(C) = −∫ Ĉ log Ĉ.
H. Discrete vs continuous care
• Normalization conventions. In the continuous case, g integrates to 1 when p integrates to 1. In the discrete (probability mass) case, ∑k g[k] = 1 as well. Verify numerically after each transform.
• Endpoints in folding. For h(d) on [0,½], endpoints 0 and ½ have measure zero in continuous math; in discrete grids, handle h[0] and h[N/2] explicitly (no doubling).
• Stability of p(θ | δ). Prefer the softmax log-sum-exp form; skip δ where g(δ) falls under a small threshold if you explicitly divide.
I. How features respond to musical transformations
• Transposition by φ:
p → shiftφ p,
g → unchanged,
M → shiftφ along θ,
C → shiftφ along θ,
(μ, ρ) → (μ ⊕ φ, ρ).
Loss implications: z-loss stays 0 under transposition; phase-loss should be computed after best θ-alignment; center-loss compares centers modulo circular distance.
• Inversion:
p(θ) → p(−θ),
g → unchanged (if folded; slightly re-ordered if directed),
M(θ, δ) → p(−θ) p(−θ ⊕ δ) = M(−θ, δ) (with appropriate column permutation if viewing δ as directed).
If inversion invariance is desired, fold δ with 1−δ and minimize phase-loss over both orientations.
J. Generative/sampling interpretations
• Two independent draws X,Y ~ p produce an interval Δ = Y ⊖ X with density g(δ) (by definition).
• Conditional anchor given an interval: p(θ | δ) ∝ p(θ) p(θ ⊕ δ).
• For “anchored” synthesis from a particular F0:
– Choose δ from a proposal (e.g., g) and optionally reweight it by context if you want conditioning;
– Choose θ from p(θ | δ);
– Map back to Hz as ftarget = f0 · 2^{m + δ} in log-chroma models (m ∈ ℤ selects the octave).
K. Choice of distances on the circle (practical math)
• L2: ∫ (a − b)^2 is simple and stable.
• JS/KL: emphasize probabilistic calibration; ensure support overlap (smoothing helps).
• 1-Wasserstein on S1: compute by unwrapping at the optimal cut (minimizing the usual 1D W1 over all cuts); more faithful when mass moves short distances on the circle.
L. Why the torus matters (disambiguating z-relations)
Because g(δ) depends only on {|ak|}, many distinct p share the same g (classic z-relation ambiguity). The torus M carries the missing phase via the identity M̂(u, v) = a_{u−v} a_v. Comparing full M (after rotation/inversion alignment) breaks the ambiguity without forfeiting transposition invariance (we align it away). In practice, z-loss encourages interval content agreement while phase-loss preserves where the intervals sit.
M. Center-field as a principled tonic estimator
The center-field C(θ) = p(θ) (W * p)(θ) rewards anchors that are both likely and surrounded by stabilizing intervals. Its first moment (μ, ρ) provides a continuous, uncertainty-aware estimate of center. When used in learning:
• Lcenter shapes the latent geometry to reflect proximity of centers.
• ρ automatically down-weights ambiguous cases and avoids penalizing musically undecidable fragments.
N. Typical numerical scales and sanity checks
• If p is normalized, then (continuous) ∫ g = 1 and (discrete) ∑ g[k] = 1.
• Column-sum property: ∑θ M(θ, δ) = g(δ) exactly (check numerically).
• If W is normalized to unit mass and p is not too spiky, ∫ C ≈ ∫ p (W * p) = (∫ p)(∫ W)(∫ p) = ∫ W ≈ constant; only the shape matters for (μ, ρ).
• After rotating p by φ, (μ, ρ) should rotate by φ and keep ρ unchanged.
O. Putting it together (compact recipe)
                                                                                 1. Build p(θ) (log-chroma density) from audio or peaks; smooth lightly; normalize.

                                                                                 2. Compute g(δ) via circular autocorrelation (iFFT of power spectrum); optionally fold.

                                                                                 3. Construct M(θ, δ) = p(θ) p(θ ⊕ δ); verify g(δ) = ∫ M(θ, δ) dθ.

                                                                                 4. Build C(θ) = p(θ) (W * p)(θ); compute (μ, ρ).

                                                                                 5. Train a feature-level VAE with
Ltotal = Lrec + βKL + λz Lz(ĝ, g) + λphase Lphase(M̂, M) + λcenter Lcenter(Ĉ, C),
with θ-alignment in Lphase, and warm-up/free-bits for KL.

                                                                                 6. Monitor that z/phase/center losses all decrease; that the learned latent places items with nearby μ close together; and that ambiguous items exhibit lower ρ and more diffuse placement.

This document is self-contained and purely mathematical: it defines all features and their identities, states the symmetries/invariances, formalizes each loss and its role, and shows how to combine them coherently to learn a center-aware latent geometry.

Goal
Produce a set of frozen, versioned feature datasets that downstream training can consume without recomputing audio front-ends or FFTs. Artifacts are small enough to store widely (except the full torus; see below).
Global IDs (for reproducibility)
                                                                                    * audio_id: stable hash of audio bytes (or path+size+mtime).

                                                                                    * cfg_id: hash of feature config (N, f_ref, smoothing kernel, fold flags, W weights, dtype).

                                                                                    * code_id: short git commit of the feature builder.
Store (audio_id, cfg_id, code_id) alongside every artifact.

Artifacts to materialize (per clip)
A0. Provenance file (JSON): audio path, sample rate, duration, (audio_id, cfg_id, code_id), split label.
A1. Peaks/salience summary (optional): compressed lists of (frequency, weight) if you use a peak-based front end.
A2. p(θ): log-CCF; shape (N,), dtype fp16 or fp32; sums to 1.
A3. P[k]: complex Fourier series of p; shape (N,), dtype complex64 (or two fp32 arrays for Re/Im).
A4. g(δ): CICV; shape (N,), dtype fp16/fp32; normalized to 1. Include both directed and folded forms if you need both.
A5. C(θ): center-field; shape (N,), dtype fp16/fp32; plus scalar (μ, ρ).
A6. Optional torus representation (choose ONE):
• Preferred: NONE (rebuild on the fly when needed).
• If you must persist something: store only P (already in A3). From P you can derive torus comparisons in Fourier (see losses). If visualization is required for a subset, store M in fp16 for those examples only.
File format and layout
                                                                                       * Use NPZ/NPY or Parquet/Arrow for simple columnar storage. For framewise variants, Zarr works well (chunk on time).

                                                                                       * Directory template: datasets/{dataset_name}/{cfg_id}/{audio_id}/
with files: p.npz, P.npz, g.npz, C.npz, mu_rho.json, provenance.json, (optional peaks.npz).

                                                                                       * Keep a manifest CSV/JSON listing all items and the absolute path to each artifact.

Disk budget (N=1024, per clip, fp32 unless noted)
                                                                                          * p: ~4 KB; P: ~8 KB; g: ~4 KB; C: ~4 KB; (μ,ρ): 16 B → ~20 KB/clip.

                                                                                          * Torus M fp16 (if stored): ~2 MB/clip → avoid globally; only store for visualization subsets.

Acceptance checks (run once after building)
                                                                                             * Rotation test: rotate audio by k semitones → p shifts by k/12, g unchanged.

                                                                                             * Normalization test: sum(p)=1, sum(g)=1 (or integral≈1), column-sum identity holds when you reconstruct M on the fly.

                                                                                             * Center-field sanity: single-center tones → high ρ; two-center mixtures → moderate ρ.

                                                                                             * Versioning: manifests record (audio_id, cfg_id, code_id); identical inputs regenerate identical bytes.

PRETRAINING / PREPROCESSING STRATEGY
Why precompute?
                                                                                                * Dramatically faster experiments: training reads small tensors (p, P, g, C, μ, ρ).

                                                                                                * Enables curriculum: start with easy targets (g, C) before adding torus-phase detail.

                                                                                                * Facilitates self-supervised tasks using transpositions/augmentations without re-running audio front-ends.

Pretraining tasks (all testable on small subsets)
PT1 — CICV prediction (interval content)
                                                                                                   * Input: p (or a lightly augmented p).

                                                                                                   * Target: g (from A4).

                                                                                                   * Loss: Lz(ĝ, g) (L2 or JS).
Goal: encoder learns interval-invariant structure first.

PT2 — Center-field regression (tonic saliency)
                                                                                                      * Input: p.

                                                                                                      * Targets: C and (μ, ρ) (from A5).

                                                                                                      * Loss: Lrec on C + center-loss on (μ, ρ).
Goal: encoder aligns features with tonic geometry.

PT3 — Phase-aware reconstruction (torus without storing M)
                                                                                                         * Inputs/Targets: operate in Fourier using P (A3).

                                                                                                         * Use the torus identity in frequency: M̂(u,v)=P[u−v]·P[v].

                                                                                                         * Define a phase-aware discrepancy that compares P̂ vs P (magnitudes + aligned phase), or compute Lphase over a small grid of θ-shifts via rephasing (no spatial M needed).
Goal: introduce placement (“where on the circle”) after PT1–PT2 converge.

PT4 — Transposition-invariant contrastive pretraining (optional)
                                                                                                            * Two “views” of the same clip: transposed p by random φ1 and φ2; optionally add detune/EQ augmentation.

                                                                                                            * Positive if from same audio (any φ), negatives from different audio.

                                                                                                            * Contrastive loss on latent after canonical alignment (e.g., rotate latent back by estimated μ).
Goal: robust invariance, stronger use of latent.

Curriculum schedule (small scale)
Stage S1 (warm-start): PT1 + PT2 only, train until metrics stabilize on a tiny synthetic set; verify latent ring emerges and (μ, ρ) correlate with labels.
Stage S2 (add placement): introduce PT3 with a small weight, increase as recon stabilizes.
Stage S3 (optional): add PT4 to improve invariance and generalization, especially for diverse timbres.
________________


FINE-TUNING / FULL OBJECTIVE (WITH PRECOMPUTED TARGETS)
Inputs to model (choose one path)
                                                                                                               * 1D path: g(δ) and/or C(θ) as primary inputs for a small 1D Conv-VAE (fastest).

                                                                                                               * 2D path: reconstruct M indirectly via P (recommended): decode P̂, then evaluate losses on ĝ (from |P̂|²) and on phase via rephasing; decode Ĉ from p̂ if the decoder also predicts p̂.

Total loss (per sample)
Ltotal = Lrec(feature)
+ β·KL (with warm-up and free-bits)
+ λz·Lz(ĝ, g)
+ λphase·Lphase(P̂, P) [phase via Fourier/rephasing, no M stored]
+ λcenter·Lcenter((μ̂,ρ̂), (μ,ρ)).
Notes
                                                                                                                  * Start with λz≈λcenter≈1, λphase small; increase λphase once Lrec is stable.

                                                                                                                  * If you prefer to reconstruct M explicitly for a subset, build M on the fly from p (outer products of shifts), not from disk.

PITFALLS & SAFEGUARDS (OFFLINE DATASETS)
                                                                                                                                 * Staleness: bump cfg_id or code_id when you change N, smoothing, W, or front-end; never mix datasets with different cfg_id in the same run.

                                                                                                                                 * Label drift: if you relabel (μ, ρ) due to updated W, only A5 must be rebuilt; A2–A4 stay valid.

                                                                                                                                 * Storage growth: do not persist M globally; store P and derive torus-related terms in Fourier for losses/visuals.

                                                                                                                                 * Evaluation leakage: ensure transposed copies of a track do not cross splits.
